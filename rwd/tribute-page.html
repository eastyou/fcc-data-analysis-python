Ada Lovelace

The first computer programmer — 1815 to 1852

A life ahead of its time

  • 1815 — Augusta Ada Byron is born in London, the daughter of the poet Lord Byron.
  • 1833 — She meets Charles Babbage and becomes fascinated by his Difference Engine.
  • 1842 — She translates Luigi Menabrea's notes on the Analytical Engine.
  • 1843 — Her extensive notes are published, including the famous Note G describing an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers.
  • 1852 — Ada dies at the age of 36, leaving behind ideas far ahead of her century.
“The Analytical Engine weaves algebraic patterns just as the Jacquard loom weaves flowers and leaves.”

Ada Lovelace imagined that a machine could do more than crunch numbers: it could manipulate symbols, compose music, and follow general-purpose instructions. That insight is why she is remembered as the first programmer.
